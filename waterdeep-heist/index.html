The Yawning Portal Just Got Chompy

General Plot Summary

The night began quietly enough at the Yawning Portal, Waterdeep's famous adventurer's tavern built atop a literal bottomless pit. One by one, a mismatched crew claimed the last open table: Murky Stormwatcher, a weathered orc ex-pirate scanning the room with quiet, dangerous confidence; Josaphine "JoJo" Battle, an 8'5" Goliath barbarian who simply shoved her way to the only open seat; Victor St. Clair, a paladin only by technicality, more interested in "connections" than commandments; and Tomothy "Moth" Ironpots, a halfling artificer whose experimental armor sparked, whirred, and promptly died the moment he needed to look impressive. Rounding out the table was Lissix Qineiros, a half-elf cleric of a wellness-and-beauty goddess who talks like she's always mid-pitch for a multi-level marketing scheme.

This mattered: strangers an hour ago, they were already a "table" — and the Yawning Portal was about to test that bond immediately.

A bar fight erupted nearby — a Kraken gang thug with an octopus tattoo throwing punches — and into the middle of it stormed Tony Frissetti, a legendary, gregarious old adventurer nobody had seen in thirty years, breaking it up with pure swagger and inviting himself to the table. Barely had introductions finished when the real threat arrived: a twelve-foot, regenerating troll wreathed in a swarm of bat-like creatures came climbing directly up out of the Yawning Portal's pit itself, right in the middle of the crowded bar.

This mattered: the party's first fight wasn't a set encounter, it was chaos erupting from the one thing everyone thought was just tavern decor.

The fight was brutal and fast: JoJo went toe-to-toe with the troll, Moth got two bats latched onto his neck and nearly went down, and Victor stood on the table to land a sneak-attack crossbow bolt. Someone finally recognized the troll's regeneration and rallied the group to focus it down before it could heal — and it went down hard, Lissix finishing it in a blast that folded the creature over, while JoJo skewered the last flying bat to the tavern wall with a thrown battleaxe.

This mattered: it welded six strangers into a party in under an hour — and left something very interesting in the corpse's throat.

Reaching into the dead troll's mouth, the party pulled out a gem shaped like a dragon's eye — clearly magical, clearly not a usable item, and (per a very good Arcana check) very clearly a key. That's when Gundren Rockseeker, an old, wealthy, and surprisingly randy gnome legend, strolled over to buy the house a round and recognized the stone on sight: it matched an artifact Tony's missing son Vinny had delivered to him just last week — right before Vinny vanished. Gundren revealed the stakes plainly: rumors of a million-gold-dragon treasure hidden somewhere in Waterdeep have suddenly gone from myth to fact, every crime family in the city is hunting for the keys, and the party is now holding one. He hired them on the spot to find Vinny. Leads then took them to Old Klebob's potion shop in the Dock/Dark Ward — where JoJo cracked the fish tank of Klebob's all-seeing fish, Sheila, nearly starting a second brawl — and on to the Skewered Dragon tavern, where a waitress named Helga, well-persuaded (and one Nat 20), revealed that Vinny was last seen with a woman tied to the Z Boys gang, and — in a detail that only came to light as the party compared notes before Session 2 — that Sheila the fish has apparently been spying on the docks for the crime lord Don Calamari all along.

This mattered: this is the moment the campaign actually started — a missing person's case that's secretly a treasure hunt, with half the underworld already circling and one of the party's own local contacts turning out to be a spy's eyes.

Key Moments & Highlights

  • The portal itself attacked them — the troll climbed straight out of the Yawning Portal's namesake pit, mid-conversation.
  • The regen-check save — a history check (after a rough Nat 1 from Tony) revealed the troll heals ~10 HP a turn, prompting the party to coordinate for the first time.
  • JoJo's axe-throw finisher, pinning the last surviving bat-creature to the tavern wall to end the fight.
  • The gemstone reveal — a 22 on an Arcana check identified the stone as a magical key, setting up the campaign's central mystery.
  • Gundren Rockseeker's entrance, casually confirming the treasure legend is real and connecting it to Tony's missing son.
  • The near-fight with Old Klebob after JoJo cracked his fish tank trying to create a distraction — rolled all the way into initiative before everyone talked it down.
  • The tavern intel chain — persuading Helga (with gold, charm, and one Nat 20) into revealing Vinny's last known movements and the Z Boys' warehouse location.

Funny & Memorable Moments

  • Tony Frissetti's entrance, complete with a flubbed walk-up cue and a full "How you doin'?" bit that became the table's favorite runner.
  • Moth's suit dying instantly on arrival, followed by his catchphrase "It's a beaut."
  • Lissix's deeply unsettling MLM-cult backstory, including a "wellness" product called Arcane Fizzle that's basically arsenic with marketing.

  • The entire Old Klebob fish tank incident — a distraction attempt that nearly became a fight over an aquarium.
  • The impromptu dice blackjack game at the bar, complete with someone trying to split a hand of ones ("no splitting, no gambling").
  • Tony shamelessly flirting information out of Helga ("you're absolutely radiant tonight") while asking about his missing son in the same breath.

Session Impact

A brand-new campaign kicked off with the party forming organically under fire rather than by design — nothing bonds strangers like co-op troll-slaying. The session also front-loaded the campaign's central hook: a literal fable-turned-fact treasure (1,000,000 gold dragons) hidden somewhere in Waterdeep, a magic key the party now possesses, and a missing-person case that's clearly more than it looks. Waterdeep's Dock/Dark Ward gang politics (Kraken Gang vs. Z Boys) were also established as a likely recurring thread.

Strategic Considerations

  • The party is level 2 — the Yawning Portal itself is recommended for level 7+ adventurers, a reminder of how far out of their depth the "real" dungeon below the tavern is.
  • The Z Boys are implicated in a kidnapping and operate out of a fortified warehouse — approach with caution rather than a straight assault.
  • The gemstone/key is clearly valuable to multiple criminal factions; keeping it secure (and quiet) should be a priority.
  • Local NPC goodwill (Klebob, Helga) was built through gold and persuasion — worth maintaining as informants, though Sheila's loyalty is now in question.

Waterdeep: Dragon Heist — Session 02

ABC Plumbing Co. and the Vinny-Shaped Hole in the Vortex

Murky Stormwatcher · Orc Rogue (Isaac) JoJo Battle · Goliath Barbarian (Joe) Victor St. Clair · Human Paladin (Jared) Moth Ironpots · Halfling Artificer (Alex) Lissix Qineiros · Elf Cleric (Nicole) Tony Frissetti · Guest (Keyan)

General Plot Summary

The night picked up right where Helga's lead left off: somewhere in the dock ward sat a warehouse marked with the Z Boys' chalk sigil — a winged snake curled into the shape of a Z. A round of investigation checks found it fast, a run-down two-story building glowing faintly from within despite the late hour. The party split its approach: Tony, Victor, and JoJo would play it straight and knock on the front door under the flimsiest of pretexts (plumbing, AC repair, "picking up a parcel" — the cover story changed by the sentence), while Murky, Moth, and Lissix grappled up to the roof to scout a skylight from above. Murky's moment on that rooftop — a quiet, unguarded beat admiring the harbor view, a nat 20 on the Wisdom check to boot — was a small, real character moment before everything went sideways. Lissix, however, promptly undercut the stealth of it all by scampering forward, dropping a vial that shattered loudly enough to echo through the whole building.

This mattered: it planted Murky's homesickness for the sea right before the party dropped into a warehouse full of corpses — and nearly blew their cover before they even got inside.

Inside was a crime scene, not an ambush: six bodies, shattered crates, a fight that had clearly already happened without them. What followed was equal parts loot run and horror show — three intact Sword Coast paintings, a rigged crate that detonated like a flashbang, a squat rack hiding a Potion of Hill Giant Strength, and long tables covered in what turned out to be an arcane drug operation (moon dust and "arcane cocaine," cooked and bagged like a meth lab dressed in wizard's robes). Lissix, trying to patch Murky up mid-search with a confident "yeah, this will probably work," instead saddled him with a nasty debuff that'll dog him the rest of the campaign — a reminder that not every vial in a drug den is medicine.

This mattered: the Z Boys' warehouse wasn't just a hideout, it was infrastructure, and somebody had already torn through it before the party arrived.

Then came Floon Blagmaar: a terrified, strawberry-blond priest who'd been kidnapped, beaten, and mistaken for Vinny. Murky pinned him to a wall at knifepoint and got the truth — Vinny had been here, tortured for information about "a treasure," chained up, and dragged off only hours before the party's arrival. It was the first hard confirmation Vinny was alive. Making it worse, the city watch showed up mid-search; Tony's silver-tongued (and nat-20-lucky) performance as a distraught, law-abiding father bought the party a clean exit, complete with Officer Brightbraid calling out a Kraken Gang tattoo on one of the corpses — confirming rival gang violence was already tearing through the docks before the party ever got there.

This mattered: it tied the warehouse slaughter to the Kraken/Z Boys gang war and cleared the party of suspicion, buying them room to keep digging.

The trail led into the sewers — a featherfall escape off the warehouse roof, a rest at the Skewered Dragon, and then a manhole dive into a tunnel that ended at a glowing whirlpool vortex. A rope-and-grapple crossing under arrow fire, a bathroom infested by a corpse-eating ooze (defeated after Tony's legendary "ABC Plumbing Co." con job scared off two thugs), and the party finally found Vinny — chained between two braziers of magical fire, Grum'Shar's boot on his chest, and Jimmy Noodles (a mind flayer lieutenant of Don Calamari) in a pinstripe suit, cradling a disembodied brain nearby. Jimmy Noodles fled the instant he clocked them. A brutal, desperate fight got Vinny stabilized and moving — punctuated by Victor's unhinged, half-panicked rant while scrambling to save Tony mid-fight — but a portal tore open and disgorged a corpse and Big Ceff, a monstrous new mind flayer enforcer, right as the party reached Vinny — forcing an all-or-nothing group leap into the vortex itself. They tumbled through and were flushed out into Waterdeep's harbor at dawn, soaked, exhausted, and — for the first time — with Vinny actually in hand.

This mattered: the rescue succeeded, but Don Calamari's organization is now aware of exactly who's been digging into his business.

Waterdeep: Dragon Heist — Session 03

Six Deeds, One Manor, and a Blue Hole of Shame

Murky Stormwatcher · Orc Rogue (Isaac) JoJo Battle · Goliath Barbarian (Joe) Victor St. Clair · Human Paladin (Jared) Moth Ironpots · Halfling Artificer (Alex) Lissix Qineiros · Elf Cleric (Nicole) Vinny Frissetti · Human Warlock (Keyan)

DM: Cole — Tony's storyline wraps up as Keyan steps into playing Vinny full-time; the crew surfaces in Waterdeep's harbor and heads to meet Gundren.

🎧 Listen to the session audio

General Plot Summary

Fresh out of the harbor and still soaked from the vortex, the party had one problem: they were now wanted men wandering hostile streets in broad daylight. Vinny — Tony's actual son, and from here on the newest full member of the crew — cast Mask of Many Faces to slip into a commoner's face, while Murky borrowed Vinny's disguise kit and, through pure bad luck, came out looking exactly like Don Calamari himself. Between that and a a full city's length to cover to reach Gundren at Trollskull Manor in the distant North Ward, the trek across Waterdeep was never going to be simple.

This mattered: Vinny's arrival gave the party real magical utility for the first time, and immediately put it to the test disguising a crew that had just made itself several enemies.

It got stranger before it got dangerous. A wrong turn dropped the party into the City of the Dead, Waterdeep's sprawling cemetery district, before Victor's nat 20 Survival check got them back on track — not before a Nat 21 Religion check at a war memorial fountain left him wreathed in a temporary Bless. Then came the real test: a pair of local toughs demanding a "toll" to cross the North Ward, backed by three more waiting to jump the party from behind. What started as a shakedown ended in a bloodbath — Moth's Magic Missile and a raging JoJo's axe cleaved two attackers apart in a single grisly exchange, and the surviving three didn't stick around once Vinny dropped his disguise, Victor's eyes lit with thaumaturgy, and Murky started ringing a bell like a man possessed. A Nat 23 intimidation check sent them running, one visibly soiling himself on the way out.

This mattered: the party is now visibly, violently building a reputation in the North Ward — for better or worse, people are going to remember them.

Dawn broke as they finally reached Trollskull Alley, paying a five-copper "toll" to three street kids guarding the stoop before walking into Trollskull Manor itself — a cobweb-choked ruin where Gundren Rockseeker sat scribbling his memoirs and Tony dozed in a rocking chair. The reunion was warm but bittersweet: Vinny confirmed his own magic item had been lost to the Zhentarim during his torture, though the party still holds the original dragon-eye stone from Session 1. Tony, having done what he came to do, gave the party a gruff, genuinely touching goodbye and headed home to his wife and daughters — closing out his arc for good.

This mattered: Tony's story wrapped cleanly, and Vinny stepped into his father's old seat as a permanent party member.

After a short rest in the sweltering, un-air-conditioned manor, the party discovered they'd inherited more than a ruin: rediscovering Floon Blagmaar squatting on their stoop (bullied by the same three street urchins — Jenks, Squidly, and a sharp-eyed deaf girl named Nat), they recruited him on the spot, adopted the kids as a scrappy "workforce," and sent them off on their first job: convince Helga to come tend bar. A full afternoon of neighborhood diplomacy followed — the block's armorer, a potion-slinging wood elf, a theatrically noir private eye named Vincent Trench, and a long-winded bookstore scholar who let slip a bombshell while pulling local records: Trollskull Manor's deed quietly changed hands overnight, to all six of them. The night ended at a rival bar, Frewn's Brews (reigning two-time "Best Bar in the North Ward"), trying to recruit the Salty Serpent pirate crew — landing the party their first real job: steal a silk handkerchief from a very fat noble and deliver it to a tiefling girl living in a box by the docks, for 400 gold.

This mattered: the party isn't just chasing Vinny's kidnappers anymore — they now own a home, a neighborhood, and a growing staff, and Waterdeep's underworld is already circling the new landlords.

  • Vinny joins the party — Mask of Many Faces disguise, charm person tricks, and a genuine warlock's toolkit replace Tony's guest-star run.
  • Murky's disguise backfires spectacularly — the "generic mobster" disguise kit makes him a dead ringer for Don Calamari himself.
  • Accidental detour through the City of the Dead — saved only by Victor's Nat 20 Survival check.

Strategic Considerations

  • The Cassalanters' guards and children have seen the party's faces. Expect a response, and don't walk back in with the same disguises.
  • Devils shrug off ordinary physical damage. Magical weapons, smites and radiant damage worked; Murky's crossbow did half.
  • The Chain Storm reaction triggers when a chain devil is bloodied, so expect big AoE damage at that threshold.
  • The glass safe is trapped with a concussive blast. Smashing it cost Murky 13 HP and alerted the house, so plan something quieter (Cole confirmed there was a way).
  • Don't drink or use the clear vials or Midnight Tears without identifying them. Victor's Arcana said evil, and Lissix called it transmutation.
  • Bag of Holding hygiene: keep the Asmodeus book isolated and unopened, and don't nest bags.
  • Gordo wants the stones. Decide whether to share the second stone's location as a partner or keep it as leverage.
  • Speculation, not confirmed: the Silver Serpent's handkerchief job fed Laertes into a Cassalanter ritual, so Gordo may already know about the villa.

Rules & Rulings From the Table

  • Weapon Mastery clarification: Mastery doesn't grant advantage. Slow cuts speed by 10 ft; Vex grants advantage on the next attack.
  • Help action on tools: a character proficient in thieves' tools can give Help to someone who isn't; Cole allowed it for the lock pick.
  • Moth's lightning launcher counts as a simple ranged weapon with an extra 1d6 once per turn, but Cole left open whether it works with off-hand attacks.
  • Natural 20 on a saving throw: Cole's ruling was to take zero damage from the Chain Storm.
  • Victor's smite can be switched off physical damage so a resistant devil takes the full amount.
  • Vinny's pact greataxe can switch damage type, but against the chain devil plain damage was best.
